--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  THE COINCIDENCE IS LIVE, TODAY, ON THE DRAFT'S OWN NUMBERS — VS's 2(a)
    worry confirmed in its worst form. Ravenwood stores monthly_rent_expense
    = 9,800 AND annual_interest_payment = 9,800 (two genuinely different
    quantities). Live turn: "Just to confirm, our annual interest payment
    is 9,800." Reply: "That matches what I have - monthly rent expense is
    $9,800." The client restated their INTEREST PAYMENT and the app named
    their RENT — a claim the client never made, spoken deterministically.
    Mechanism: _figures_all_on_file walks the state dict and takes the
    FIRST leaf whose value matches (first-leaf-wins); the client's own
    words are never consulted.
    CENSUS (artifact-level): 4,334 of 4,674 real drafts (92.7%) carry at
    least one value >= 1000 stored under two or more different leaf names.
    Most collisions are derived twins (current_cogs == cogs_total_year1,
    current_payroll == payroll_total_year1 == baseline_payroll_year1) where
    naming either is harmless-ish, but genuinely unrelated pairs are real:
    e.g. draft 005705e0: current_capex == owner_compensation == 280,000;
    Ravenwood's own rent == interest. The 1000 floor does NOT make the
    coincidence rare — it is the NORM.
    RULING ON THE FLOOR (VS asked): the floor is the wrong instrument for
    this defect — it guards against small-figure ambiguity and does that
    fine (A3: sub-1000 never matches). Keep the floor; fix the NAMING.
    FIX SHAPE (deterministic, no NLP): name a field ONLY when exactly one
    DISTINCT leaf name matches the value. Multiple distinct names ->
    speak the value without a field claim: "That matches a figure I have
    on file - $9,800." One name (the common case: current_revenue) ->
    name it as today. This kills the false claim without touching the
    good case; VS's router-domain candidate is heavier and not needed.
